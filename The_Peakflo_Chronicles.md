# The Peakflo Chronicles: Unlocking the Cash Trap

**Date:** May 22, 2024
**To:** Executive Leadership team

## The Tale of Two Customers

Meet **"Prompt Paul"**. Paul runs a mid-sized agency. He pays every invoice on Day 28. He’s boring, reliable, and represents **35% of our revenue**. 

Now meet **"Chaotic Charlie"**. Charlie runs a fast-growing startup. He pays, eventually. Usually 15 days late. He loses emails. He forgets due dates.

**The Surprising Finding:**
For the last two years, our AR team has been treating Paul and Charlie exactly the same.
*   We send Paul a "Just Checking In" email on Day 31. He gets annoyed because he already scheduled the wire.
*   We send Charlie a "Just Checking In" email on Day 31. He missed it because he's drowning in email.

**The Human Cost:**
Our AR team is burned out. They spend 40 hours a week chasing 500 customers, sending the same generic templates to everyone. They feel like "bad guys" nagging good customers, while the actual bad guys (the 112 "Ghost" accounts) slip through the cracks.

## The 15-Day Gap (The "Aha!" Moment)

When we re-built the AR timeline transaction-by-transaction, a startling pattern emerged. 
There is a massive segment of **153 customers** (The "Slow but Steady" cohort) who consistently pay exactly **12-16 days late**.

This isn't a cash flow problem for them. It's a **workflow problem**. 
They pay when they do their mid-month check run. 

**The Insight:**
We are chasing them for cash they *intend* to pay, but we aren't giving them the right cue.
*   **The Fix:** We don't need a collections agent to call them. We just need to change the due date on their invoice or send an automated nudge 3 days *after* the due date, catching them right before their check run.

## The "Ghost" in the Machine

While everyone was worried about Charlie being 2 weeks late, the real villain was hiding in plain sight.
**112 Accounts** have accumulated **$1.8M** in debt and have **never made a single payment** in the last 6 months of data history.

**The Shock:** 
Some of these accounts were still receiving "Friendly Reminder" emails.
This is where the human element failed. The "Gut Feeling" said "Give them time, they are big accounts." The Data says: **They are gone.**

## Wisdom vs. Intelligence (The ML Experiment)

We trained an advanced AI model (Random Forest) to predict payment dates. It was brilliant. It knew that "Large Invoices issued in December" would be paid 43 days late.

But we decided **not** to use it.
why? Because the AI couldn't explain *why* it flagged a customer. And in finance, if you can't explain it, you can't trust it.

Instead, we chose a simple, human-readable rule: *"If they miss 3 payments in a row, stop the service."* 
It’s not as "smart" as the AI, but it’s **wiser**. Use the AI to alert us, but use the Rule to act.

## The Happy Ending (Projected)

By automating the "Nudge" for Charlie and cutting off the "Ghosts", we don't just fix the spreadsheet.
*   **We free the AR Team**: To focus on high-value relationships.
*   **We respect Paul**: By leaving him alone.
*   **We unlock $1.5M**: That was previously trapped in "Waiting Mode".

Data isn't just about numbers. It's about understanding the behavior of the humans sending the checks.
